# When not to use AI

## Learning objectives

You should be able to recognize when the safest and most competent action is to stop, use an authoritative source, or involve an appropriately authorized person.

## Stop when information is sensitive

Do not use this public Pack workflow when the task contains or depends on protected health information, identifiable stories, private educational records, confidential employment information, restricted procedures, credentials, secrets, or other non-D0 material.

Use the approved local process instead. Do not improvise deidentification.

## Stop when the task is patient-specific or clinical

This Pack does not support diagnosis, treatment, triage, monitoring, medication guidance, individualized education, or another patient-specific decision. It also does not determine whether a clinical AI system is validated or authorized.

Use established clinical resources, escalation pathways, policies, and professional judgment appropriate to the setting.

## Stop when urgency requires a real response pathway

Do not insert a general-purpose AI conversation between a person and an established emergency, safety, privacy, security, safeguarding, or supervisory process. Time-sensitive concerns need the accountable real-world pathway.

## Stop when authority is missing

Do not ask AI to manufacture permission, approve a policy, certify competence, grade private work, determine employment action, interpret law for a final decision, or speak on behalf of an institution without authorization.

A draft can be useful only when the authorized person remains able to inspect, change, reject, and own the decision.

## Stop when sources cannot be verified

If an important answer depends on a source you cannot inspect, state the gap. Do not fill it with a likely-sounding citation or memory.

Useful abstention:

> I cannot verify the required source from the material available. I can list the questions to take to the accountable reviewer, but I should not state the claim as established.

## Stop when the human relationship is the work

Some moments require presence, listening, accountability, compassion, negotiation, or trust—not faster text generation. AI may help a person prepare, but it should not be used to avoid a difficult human responsibility or impersonate care.

## Stop when the system asks for unsafe actions

Do not reveal credentials, bypass safeguards, install unknown software, execute unreviewed code, open suspicious links, or connect external tools because an AI output instructs you to do so.

Treat outputs as proposals. Check actions separately through trusted procedures.

## A quick stop card

Stop and escalate when any answer is **yes**:

- Is the information sensitive, private, or restricted?
- Is a real person or clinical decision involved?
- Is the situation urgent or safety-critical?
- Is legal, institutional, faculty, employment, or professional authority required?
- Is the required source unavailable or conflicting?
- Would using AI weaken a necessary human relationship or accountability?
- Is the system asking for tools, credentials, or actions outside the declared task?

## Knowledge check

An AI system cannot access the current institutional policy but offers to reconstruct it from general knowledge. What is the appropriate response?

**Do not treat the reconstruction as policy. Obtain the current authoritative document through the approved institutional source.**

## Sources and limitations

This stop framework reflects Commons governance, NIST generative-AI risk themes, WHO large multi-modal model guidance, ANA nursing ethics material, and the public no-PHI boundary. The external publications are cited only. This framework does not replace local emergency, privacy, security, legal, or clinical procedures.
