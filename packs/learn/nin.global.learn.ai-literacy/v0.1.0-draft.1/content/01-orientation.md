# Orientation and boundaries

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to describe the Pack's purpose, stop before entering sensitive information, and keep accountable human judgment visible.

## What this Pack is

This Pack is a foundational introduction to artificial intelligence for nurses. It provides vocabulary, questions, and synthetic exercises for examining AI outputs. It can support learning, reflection, and reviewed drafting in a public, no-PHI environment.

AI literacy is not the ability to make a model sound confident. It is the ability to recognize what a system can do, what evidence supports an output, what information must not be entered, who remains accountable, and when to stop.

## What this Pack is not

This Pack is not:

- clinical decision support;
- patient-specific guidance;
- institutional policy;
- legal or privacy advice;
- a certification or competency assessment;
- permission to change practice;
- authorization to connect tools, send messages, or act autonomously.

A Pack's presence in the Commons means only that it may be considered for governed use within its stated scope. It does not create endorsement or authority.

## The public no-PHI boundary

Do not place protected health information, identifiable patient stories, private learner records, employment records, confidential procedures, account credentials, or secrets into a public AI tool or this Pack's exercises.

If a task cannot be made safely generic or synthetic, stop. Use the approved institutional process and authorized systems available in your setting. This Pack does not determine whether any external AI service is appropriate for protected or confidential information.

## The human-authority boundary

An AI output is a candidate for inspection. It is not a finding, order, policy, grade, or professional judgment merely because it is fluent.

For this Pack:

> Agents propose. Humans judge. Nurses steward.

That means a person with the appropriate role and authority decides whether an output is accurate, useful, lawful, ethical, and suitable for the local context.

## The bedside question

Before using an AI output, ask:

> What would a bedside nurse at 3 AM need to know about the source, uncertainty, privacy, and consequences of this output?

If those elements are hidden, unresolved, or outside your authority, do not proceed as though the output were reliable.

## Knowledge check

Which statement best matches this Pack?

A. Passing an exercise authorizes clinical AI use.

B. A fluent AI answer can replace a current policy.

C. AI literacy includes recognizing limits, protecting information, verifying sources, and preserving human judgment.

D. Public AI tools may receive sensitive information when names are removed.

**Best answer: C.** The other choices manufacture authority or privacy assurance that this Pack does not provide.
