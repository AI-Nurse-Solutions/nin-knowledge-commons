# Human judgment and accountability

## Learning objectives

You should be able to name who must decide, what information that person needs, and why a rubber-stamp review is inadequate.

## Assistance does not transfer accountability

AI can help generate, organize, compare, or retrieve candidate information. It cannot assume a nurse's license, faculty role, employer authority, duty to a person, or accountability for consequences.

The existence of a human somewhere in a process is not enough. Effective human review requires:

- competence appropriate to the decision;
- access to the relevant evidence and context;
- enough time and attention to inspect the output;
- authority to reject, correct, or stop;
- freedom from misleading automation pressure;
- a clear record of what was decided and why when consequences warrant it.

## Name the decision

Before using AI, finish this sentence:

> The AI may help ________, but ________ remains responsible for deciding ________ under ________ authority.

If the blanks cannot be filled honestly, the workflow lacks a visible accountability path.

## Review is not approval theater

Clicking “accept,” copying an output, or adding a person's name does not prove meaningful review. The reviewer should know:

- what the system was asked to do;
- what information it received;
- what sources support the output;
- known limitations and uncertainty;
- what changed after human review;
- what consequences may follow;
- when escalation or abstention is required.

## Contributors, reviewers, institutions, and stewards

The Commons separates roles:

- contributors propose content;
- reviewers verify only within declared competence and scope;
- institutions authorize local use within their own authority;
- nurses steward safe, accountable use;
- AI systems may assist but cannot approve themselves.

A reviewer statement does not automatically create accreditation, certification, clinical validation, or authorization for another institution.

## A synthetic example

An educator uses AI to draft three discussion questions from a public reading. The educator checks each question against the reading, removes an unsupported assumption, adjusts language for the learners, and decides whether the questions fit the course.

The AI assisted drafting. The educator made the educational decision. This does not imply that an AI system may grade private learner work or determine competence.

## Knowledge check

What is the strongest sign of meaningful human oversight?

A. A human clicked the final button.

B. The system says a human is responsible.

C. A qualified person had the evidence, context, time, and authority to reject or stop the output.

D. The output was generated by a well-known vendor.

**Best answer: C.** Oversight must be capable, informed, and consequential.
